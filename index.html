Flow Content Plan

Prepared for Jack Davies · July 2026 · Covers the three revenue-critical lifecycle flows: Quiz Complete, Quiz Started, Abandoned Checkout

MXT collects 136 data points per person and sells clinically-dosed, personalized routines. The emails should be impossible to mistake for anyone else's — because no one else can write them. This plan defines the voice, the enemy, and every message in the three flows that decide whether a lead becomes a subscriber.

01

The voice layer — enemy, proof language, signature elements

Applied across every email and SMS in every flow below.

The enemy: mass-produced skincare

MXT never punches at a named competitor. The enemy is the shelf — products formulated for a statistical average that doesn't exist. Every email gets its tension from this contrast, and the personalization data is the weapon.

"Made for everyone. Which means no one. Which means not you." The anti-shelf line — the core enemy frame. Mass products are averaged; an average is a person who doesn't exist.
"Everything and the lab sink." The kitchen-sink label attack: 40 ingredients at trace doses so the box reads impressive — nothing at a concentration that works. Sets up MXT's counter: a few actives, at effective doses, chosen for you.
"It's not just what's in your skincare. It's what's missing — and what should be missing." The subtraction principle. Personalization is also exclusion: fragrance out for reactive skin, pore-cloggers out for congestion, redundant actives out so the ones that matter can work. Only possible when you know the person. This is MXT's most defensible copy idea — nobody selling off a shelf can say it.

Three signature elements (every email carries at least one)

ElementHow it shows up
STATSEvery message carries at least one hard number: 136 data points · a 9.5 UV index · 5.0% glycolic · weeks 8–12 · $20. A data-driven brand that writes in adjectives is out of character; numbers are MXT's native tongue.
CLINICAL FORMULATIONMechanism-first copy: name the active, the dose, the pathway, the timeline. "Formulated, not merchandised." The tone of a lab that read your file — never influencer-speak.
CUSTOM CONCENTRATIONSThe profile already stores each person's exact ingredient percentages ({product_key_ingredient_1_percent}-1 → "5.0%"). Printing your doses in your email is the proof no shelf brand can copy — the single most underused asset in the account.

The competitive frame

Direct competitors — Prose, Function of Beauty, Geologie — are never named in emails. We name the practice instead:

  • vs mass brands (the open enemy): averaged formulas, kitchen-sink labels, "made for everyone but you."
  • vs other quiz brands (the implicit contrast): "Some brands personalize the label. We personalize the formula — and the exclusions." MXT's edges: skincare-specific depth (130+ points vs a 5-question widget), exact per-person concentrations, and environment intelligence (UV / air quality / water hardness from the zip code) that no quiz-to-bestseller funnel touches.
Stats we need from you (they make the copy hit harder): ① how many unique routine permutations the engine can produce (the "we could never stock this on a shelf" number) · ② the formulation exclusion list — what MXT refuses to put in a bottle and why · ③ typical shelf-product concentrations vs MXT doses for the top actives · ④ substantiation for the results timeline (weeks 2/4/8/12).
02

What we found in the current flows

Full message-by-message pull of the live flows, July 15. The architecture is directionally right — the execution is what's hurting.

  • Placeholder text is reaching customers. Several live emails and most flow SMS contain literal {Primary Concern} / {First Name} as visible text (unwired duplicate content blocks), and the mini-quiz flow opens with literal "ROUTINE/PRODUCT … SKINCONCERN."
  • Mismatched science. The ingredient-education email pairs Product 2 with Product 1's ingredient description — the credibility email currently contradicts itself.
  • The two most persuasion-critical emails are image-only (social proof; the trial de-risk email) — invisible to anyone with images off, and a spam-filter red flag that feeds the open-rate problem directly.
  • Two flows are mailing the same audience. "Trial Non-Conversion Nurture" and "Quiz Complete — No Purchase" both target quiz-completers who didn't buy: up to ~12 emails + 12 SMS in three weeks to a brand-new list. Likely a meaningful contributor to the deliverability slide. (Trigger config to be confirmed in the UI.)
  • Manufactured urgency. "Your quiz results will expire and you'll need to retake the quiz" — a claim this audience can test, and one that costs trust the brand's whole premise depends on.
  • SMS is built but has never sent (no sending setup) — and several texts are missing opt-out language, deep-link to the homepage instead of the results page, or repeat each other verbatim.
  • No active checkout-abandonment coverage. The highest-intent audience in the account currently receives nothing — Flow C below is that build.
  • The environment goldmine is untouched. UV index, seasonality, air quality, water hardness — generated for every completed quiz, used in zero emails.
03

The method: one rung at a time

Nobody leaps from "took a quiz" to "$99/month subscriber." Each flow meets the person at the last thing they actually did, and scaffolds exactly one next step — the step just beyond what they'd do on their own (their zone of proximal development):

Where they stoppedThe one rung we ask forWhat we never ask for here
Started the quiz, didn't finishFinish the quiz (2 minutes)Money — that's two rungs up
Finished the quiz, didn't buyTry it — $20, reversibleThe $99/mo commitment
Started checkout, didn't payFinish the purchase they already choseRe-persuasion (it plants doubt)

The subscription is won later — by the product, the onboarding, and the retinoid-timeline education. These three flows exist to start trials, full stop.

TRAFFIC ──▶ Quiz Started ──▶ Quiz Complete ──▶ Checkout Started ──▶ Trial ──▶ Subscription │ │ │ ▼ ▼ ▼ [ FLOW B ] [ FLOW A ] [ FLOW C ] finish quiz start trial finish checkout Priority: C ▸ A ▸ B — each flow EXITS the moment the person levels up (B exits on quiz-complete; A exits on checkout-started; all three exit on Placed Order). One flow per person at a time.
Flow A

Quiz Complete — No Purchase

6 emails + 3 SMS · ~10 days
Goal: start a $20 trial.  Audience: completed the full quiz, saw their routine, didn't add to cart — where all paid traffic lands.  Arc: validate → explain → prove → de-risk → decide.
SMS 1 · 20 minutes

"Your results are saved"

Message
"Mxt: Your {skin_profile} analysis is saved — see your {routine_name_1}: [results deep-link]. Reply STOP to opt out."
ZPD
Anchoring — consolidate the learning they just finished before it fades.
Why
SMS is the one channel guaranteed to arrive right now, and the results page is the strongest seller the brand owns. No pitch — we're returning something that's theirs.
Email 1 · 1 hour

"Your skin, decoded"

Narrative
Open with their {skin_profile} sentence verbatim, then their concerns ({concern_name}-1/-2), then the routine card with {routine_formula_rationale}. Read-back first, products second. Near-plain-text build.
ZPD
Validation — confirm the self-knowledge they just earned before asking for anything new.
Why
At today's open rates only self-referential relevance earns the open, and a mature audience trusts being understood before being sold. Their own words are the one subject line they can't ignore.
Flavor
STAT: "We analyzed 136 data points about your skin." Enemy (soft): "This wasn't pulled off a shelf — it couldn't be."
Email 2 · Day 2

"Every ingredient has a job — and yours are dosed for you"

Narrative
Per product: the key active ({product_key_ingredient_1_name}-N) at its exact concentration ({product_key_ingredient_1_percent}-N), what it does for {concern_name}-1 — and one line on what was left out and why (sensitivity-aware via {skin_sensitivity_level}).
ZPD
Comprehension — bridge from "this is mine" to "I understand why it would work on me."
Why
A 45+/55+ buyer with wrinkle and firmness concerns purchases mechanism, not vibes — actives, percentages, pathways. This is also where the current flow's mismatched tags get fixed; the science email must not contradict itself.
Flavor
CLINICAL FORMULATION + CUSTOM CONCENTRATIONS centerpiece. Enemy: "Mass-market serums are everything and the lab sink — 40 ingredients, none at a dose that matters. Your routine runs a handful of actives at working concentrations. And just as deliberately, some things are missing."
Email 3 · Day 4

"We also checked where you live"

Narrative
{location_city_state} + the AI-written {uv_index_description} and {stressor_water_hardness} narrative → why the routine includes {product_spf_note}-1. "You told us 20 things. Your zip code told us 10 more."
ZPD
Relevance expansion — deepen "built for me" with data they didn't consciously give.
Why
The "how did they know that?" moment — the one personalization a shelf product structurally cannot make, and it sets up the SPF logic (retinoids + high UV) as dermatology rather than a pitch.
Flavor
STATS: the actual UV index and air-quality numbers for their zip. Enemy: "A shelf product doesn't know it's a 9.5-UV summer in Pasadena. Yours does."
Email 4 · Day 6

Proof from people with their exact concern

Narrative
Testimonials filtered to {concern_name}-1 — the named-customer quotes already written for the nurture flow (they're good), rebuilt as real text, with the results-timeline frame (weeks 2/4/8).
ZPD
Vicarious mastery — belief through peers one rung ahead of them.
Why
No founder story, no influencers — concern-matched peer proof is the only social scaffold this brand has, and matching it keeps the personalization promise unbroken. Replaces the current image-only proof email that spam filters can't read.
Flavor
STATS: timeline numbers inside the quotes ("noticeable by week 3"). Age-adjacent attribution where possible — peers should look like the list (45+).
Email 5 · Day 8 — the decision email

"Two ways to try. Zero ways to get trapped."

Narrative
$20 trial-with-subscription (save $30) vs $50 one-time, side by side, genuinely neutral. What's physically in the box. Cancel-anytime shown as three steps. Sensitivity ramp note via {skin_sensitivity_level}.
ZPD
The guarded step — shrink the leap to a $20 reversible action, escape valve visible.
Why
The funnel's known leak is offer fear; showing the one-time path makes the subscription feel safe, which is exactly what converts the wary majority to the cheaper subscription trial. Sell the trial. Never the $99.
Flavor
CLINICAL: frame the trial as "a patch test for a whole routine." STAT: the $30 delta does the talking.
Email 6 · Day 10

Honest last call

Narrative
Recap card + {free_reward} if present. A real deadline if one exists (gift window, price change) — otherwise "your results stay saved" and a graceful exit to long-term nurture. The fake "your formula expires, retake the quiz" line is retired.
ZPD
Closure — urgency only after every belief rung is built, never as a substitute for one.
Why
Manufactured expiry reads as a lie the moment someone tests it — and trust is the entire brand premise. An honest close keeps the list mailable for the repriced offer that's coming.
SMS 2 · Day 8 (with Email 5)  ·  SMS 3 · Day 10

Decision + last call mirrors

Message
"$20 trial (save $30) or $50 one-time — your {concern_name}-1 routine, your call: [deep-link]" · then one short last-call. Every SMS carries STOP language and deep-links to their results, never the homepage.
Flow B

Quiz Started — Not Completed

4 emails (+1 conditional SMS) · ~6 days · NEW build
Goal: finish the quiz — not buy. Their profile is mostly empty; this flow's job is to create the personalization every other flow spends.  Exit: the moment quiz-complete fires (hand off to Flow A).
Email 1 · 30–60 minutes

"Your skin analysis is unfinished"

Narrative
Resume-where-you-left-off link. "About 2 minutes left." Nothing else — no products, no prices.
ZPD
Task completion — the smallest possible next step, with the path restored (resume, not restart).
Why
Mid-task abandonment is usually interruption, not rejection, and intent decays by the hour. Mentioning money here would be asking for a rung they can't reach yet.
Flavor
STAT: "You're ~60% through. 2 minutes left."
Email 2 · Day 1

"Here's what's waiting at the end"

Narrative
Show the artifact: a sample (anonymized) results page — the skin-profile paragraph, the UV/environment card, a 3-product routine with concentrations. "This is what people who finish walk away with. It's free."
ZPD
Motivation — raise the visible reward, since they've already priced the effort.
Why
People love learning about themselves — the results page is a powerful seller in its own right (your words on the call, and the data agrees). Sell the results page; the results page sells the routine.
Flavor
Enemy: "A 5-question quiz that ends in a bestseller isn't an analysis — it's a sales funnel with a costume on. This one runs 130+ data points." CLINICAL: the sample page shows real percentages.
Email 3 · Day 3

The easier rung: the 30-second version

Narrative
"Short on time? Take the 30-second version." Mini quiz as the primary CTA, full quiz alongside. No guilt about the abandon.
ZPD
Difficulty reduction — when a learner stalls on a rung, you lower the rung; you don't repeat the instruction louder.
Why
The mini-quiz path and its results flow already exist, so this costs nothing — and a mini-quiz completer generates enough data to enter personalized nurture instead of dying in a dead-end segment.
Email 4 · Day 6

"What most people your age discover"

Narrative
The honest aggregate: "the most common discoveries among people who finish — fine lines they'd started ignoring, sun exposure their zip code explains, a routine mismatch they'd never have guessed." Final resume link, then graceful exit to the general list.
ZPD
Normative pull — the last lever when task- and reward-scaffolds haven't moved them.
Why
The aggregate is real (wrinkles and firmness dominate this list), so the curiosity gap is honest — and a clean exit protects the sender reputation instead of shouting at non-responders.
Flavor
STATS: the concern distribution, told as story ("2 in 3 people who finish are treating a concern they almost never say out loud").
Conditional SMS · Day 2

Resume nudge — only if a phone exists

Message
"Your Mxt skin analysis is ~60% done — finish in 2 min: [resume link]. Reply STOP to opt out."
Why
The phone question sits at the end of the quiz, so most abandoners won't have one on file — this step fires for the few who do. Worth confirming where the phone capture sits; moving it earlier is a separate conversation with real trade-offs.
Flow C

Abandoned Checkout

4 emails + 2 SMS · ~4 days · NEW build
Goal: complete the purchase they already chose. Highest-intent audience in the account — the barrier is trust and interruption, not comprehension.  Rule: no discounts. Pricing is being rebuilt separately, and training a two-week-old list to abandon-for-coupons is permanent damage.
SMS 1 · 25 minutes

State restore

Message
"Mxt: Your trial kit is still in your cart — {routine_name_1}, saved and ready: [checkout deep-link]. Reply STOP to opt out."
ZPD
Friction removal — resumption, zero re-decision.
Why
Most checkout abandons are interruptions, especially for this demographic. The fastest recovery is the channel that actually arrives today.
Email 1 · 1 hour

"You were 30 seconds from done"

Narrative
A receipt of their own decision: the routine card of exactly what's in the cart, the $20 trial price restated, one CTA back to checkout. No new arguments.
ZPD
Same rung, cleared path — they already climbed to "decided"; don't re-teach, re-open the door.
Why
Re-persuading someone who already chose introduces doubt where there was none. The email's whole job is to make resumption effortless.
Email 2 · Day 1 — the load-bearing email

"First retinoid routine? Here's exactly what to expect"

Narrative
Name the two unspoken fears and dismantle both: "will it hurt my skin?" → the week-1 tingle explained, the ramp matched to {skin_sensitivity_level}, and what was excluded from their formula because of what they told us; "am I trapped?" → cancel-anytime in three shown steps, what happens after the trial, transparent subscription mechanics.
ZPD
Security scaffold — the block is emotional, not informational, so the scaffold is safety.
Why
A 55-year-old first-time retinoid buyer at an unknown brand's subscription checkout has rational fears; naming them is what a trusted clinician does. The best barrier-education copy in the current account gets harvested into this send.
Flavor
Subtraction principle, deployed at the moment it matters most: "Your formula is also defined by what's not in it — no [fragrance/known trigger], because you told us your skin reacts. That's not a product line. That's your file."
Email 3 · Day 2

The two-paths math

Narrative
$20 trial-with-subscription (save $30) vs $50 one-time — side by side, both CTAs live, genuinely neutral: "either way, you try it. One path just costs less because you let us plan ahead."
ZPD
Choice architecture — control over the terms is the final rung before commitment for a wary buyer.
Why
The one-time option is the pressure-release valve by design; showing it honestly makes the subscription feel safe rather than sneaky — which is what actually moves people onto the cheaper subscription path.
Flavor
STATS carry the email: two prices, one delta, zero adjectives.
SMS 2 · Day 2, evening

Short last call

Message
"Still yours: the {concern_name}-1 trial kit, $20. Checkout takes about a minute: [link]. Reply STOP to opt out."
Email 4 · Day 4

"Your cart expires. Your routine doesn't."

Narrative
Release the pressure without ending the relationship: the cart may clear, the results and formula stay saved. Exit into Flow A's nurture tail.
ZPD
Closure without regression — a "no" is a "not yet"; keep them on the ladder.
Why
With repricing weeks away, the worst outcome isn't a lost sale — it's a burned contact. Someone who abandons at $50 today may convert at the new price, but only if these four days felt respectful.

Flow architecture — how the three coexist

FlowTriggerSuppression / exit (all conditions AND'd)
C — CheckoutCheckout StartedPlaced Order = 0 since entry
A — Quiz CompleteQuiz-complete eventPlaced Order = 0 AND Checkout Started = 0 since entry → hands off to C
B — Quiz StartedQuiz-start eventQuiz Complete = 0 AND Placed Order = 0 since entry → hands off to A
  • One flow per person at a time. Priority C ▸ A ▸ B; each flow exits the moment the person levels up.
  • Consolidate the parallel nurture. The existing second quiz-completer flow either merges its best content into Flow A or is re-scoped to a genuinely different audience — one flow owns each audience.
  • Suppress on the downstream goal, never the trigger event; negatives joined by AND — the two silent killers (a flow that empties, a flow that over-sends) both live in these filters.
  • Sending posture during deliverability recovery: these flows target recent, engaged people — safe to send and reputation-positive. Broad sends stay off until the domain recovers.
