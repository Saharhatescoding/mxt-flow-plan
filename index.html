01

What has to happen — in order

  1. Fix deliverability.Opens are at ~8% and falling. Authentication (SPF / DKIM / DMARC + a dedicated sending domain), engaged-only sending, and plain-text warm-up campaigns to the most-engaged leads. Nothing else in this document lands in an inbox until this is done. Recovery window: 2–4 weeks.
  2. Get the core flow live: Abandoned Checkout.The highest-intent audience in the account currently receives nothing. Built new (3 emails + 3 SMS below), suppressed correctly, live before ad spend scales.
  3. Rebuild Quiz Complete with personalized content.The flagship flow — every paid lead lands here. The quiz engine writes ~40 finished copy blocks per person; the rebuild wires them in (see the coverage table below).
  4. Launch Quiz Started — Not Completed.New flow. Email is captured before the quiz finishes, and some abandoners already carry a primary concern — so the flow branches: concern-captured vs. email-only. The one job is finishing the quiz, which creates the data every other flow spends.
  5. Turn SMS on.Numbers and consent are already collected at quiz end; nothing sends today. SMS is the bridge channel while email deliverability recovers (10DLC/TFN registration check first). Every flow below carries an SMS spine.
Offer alignment (found during the audit): the live site sells "Try Your Routine Free — just $6.95 S&H," converting to the full routine 21 days after arrival — but every existing email and SMS still pushes "$20 trial (save $30) or $50 one-time." The email program is selling an offer that no longer exists. Every message in this plan pushes the real one: free trial pack · 21 days · $6.95 shipping. No subscription-objection copy anywhere; price appears only as a stated fact on CTAs.

The facts, in brief

  • Coverage: 1,962 profiles · 1,146 (58%) carry a full routine recommendation · 977 (50%) carry an age — the personalization fuel already exists for the majority of the list.
  • Mature audience: 62% are 45+, 46% are 55+. Default voice = mature-skin, results-and-science; the acne/oily/pores minority (~15%) branches off {concern_name}-1 so nobody gets the wrong story.
  • The retinoid engine: Retinoate + Bakuchiol appear together on 1,306 recommendation slots — the Tri-Retin line is the workhorse. Retinoid education (the sensitivity ramp, the SPF pairing, the weeks 8–12 timeline) is core copy, not a nice-to-have.

  • Environment layer: every completed quiz generates zip-derived UV index, air quality, water hardness and seasonality plus pre-written narrative paragraphs — 10 properties, zero current usage, all deployed in A-E3.
  • Quiz abandoners are not blank: profiles sync progressively — some carry a primary concern before finishing, some only an email. That split is exactly Flow B's branch.

  • Consolidate the parallel nurture: the existing second quiz-completer flow merges its best content into Flow A (its concern-matched testimonials and barrier education are the strongest copy in the account) — one flow owns each audience.

07

Appendix — the voice layer: enemy, proof language, signature elements

The copy system behind every message above.

The enemy: mass-produced skincare

MXT never punches at a named competitor. The enemy is the shelf — products formulated for a statistical average that doesn't exist.

"Made for everyone. Which means no one. Which means not you." The anti-shelf line — mass products are averaged; an average is a person who doesn't exist.
"Everything and the lab sink." The kitchen-sink label attack: 40 ingredients at trace doses so the box reads impressive — nothing at a concentration that works.
"It's not just what's in your skincare. It's what's missing — and what should be missing." The subtraction principle. Personalization is also exclusion: fragrance out for reactive skin, pore-cloggers out for congestion. Only possible when you know the person. Deployed at the highest-stakes moment: C-E2.

Three signature elements (every email carries at least one)

ElementHow it shows up
STATSEvery message carries at least one hard number: 20 answers · a 9.7 UV index · 2.0% salicylic · 21 days · $6.95. Numbers are the brand's native tongue.
CLINICAL FORMULATIONMechanism-first copy: the active, the dose, the pathway, the timeline. "Formulated, not merchandised."
CUSTOM CONCENTRATIONSThe profile stores each person's exact ingredient percentages ({product_key_ingredient_1_percent}-1 → "2.0%"). Printing your doses in your email is the proof no shelf brand can copy — currently used zero times.

The competitive frame

Prose, Function of Beauty, Geologie are never named in emails — we name the practice: "Some brands personalize the label. We personalize the formula — and the exclusions." MXT's edges: 130+ data points vs a 5-question widget, exact per-person concentrations, and environment intelligence no quiz-to-bestseller funnel touches.

Stats we need from Jack: ① unique routine permutations the engine can produce · ② the formulation exclusion list (what MXT refuses to bottle, and why) · ③ typical shelf concentrations vs MXT doses for the top actives · ④ substantiation for the results timeline · ⑤ whether the free-reward window is enforced (for C-E3's deadline).
